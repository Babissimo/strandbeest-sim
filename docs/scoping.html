Strandbeest Simulator Scoping

A browser simulator for Evolving Machines (three pairs of Year 11/12 students, Tue 27 to Thu 29 Oct), built on Fri 23, Sat 24 and Mon 26 Oct. This page records what I checked, the pieces, an order against those dates, the risks, and the decisions that are yours to make (§5).

§1What I checked

Hosting

babissimo.net is a Quarto site on GitHub Pages. Each project lives in its own repo on its own subdomain (DNS at Cloudflare) with a card in projects/projects.yml. There are two deploy patterns: Ratchet's page is a Cloudflare Worker serving a public/ folder (wrangler, custom domain, a CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN secret), while Torus Chess and World Happiness are GitHub Pages sites with a CNAME. The difference that matters here is that Cloudflare can set response headers and GitHub Pages cannot (Q9, Q12).

Runtime

PyScript 0.7.31 (July 2026) loads Pyodide 314.0.2, which runs Python 3.14.2. Pyodide does not bundle pymunk, but pymunk 7.3.1 publishes WebAssembly wheels on PyPI and PyScript installed one from there. In the browser on your Mac, Python, numpy and pymunk were ready 4.4 s after page start, after a download of about 9 MB. PyScript also ships a CodeMirror editor (py-editor) with Python highlighting. Every host involved (jsDelivr, pypi.org, files.pythonhosted.org) allows cross-origin loading, so the page can be cross-origin isolated if the host sets the headers.

Two traps in the jam rule

The teleport is common. Of 300 jam-free random legs at each spread, 6% (±10%), 14% (±25%) and 20% (±50%) moved a joint more than 10 mm in one 2° crank step, against 1.9 mm for Jansen. Evolution will find and exploit these (Q14).

Physics arena spike

A throwaway pymunk model of a whole beest (each leg is two rigid triangles and four bars, pinned to a chassis and a motor-driven crank) walks in the right direction and agrees with the kinematic model for the six-legged beest:

BeestKinematic, mm/turnArena, mm/turn
1 pair125.9tipped over
2 pairs at 180°135.3148.0
3 pairs at 120°141.1141.9–144.7

That needs about 600 physics steps per crank turn: at 240 the joints went soft and the beest fell apart. Results were identical in CPython and the browser, so arena runs are repeatable.

§5Questions for you

Q1–Q11 are the handover's own numbers; Q12 onwards are new. They are grouped by when the build needs the answer.

Needed Friday morning

Q1

Where do scores come from: geometry, geometry with a tipping check, the arena, or geometry to evolve and the arena to test and race?

Found: the geometry model is roughly 20 to 200 times cheaper per beest than an arena trial, depending on pure Python or numpy (chart in §1). The arena agrees with the kinematics for six legs, reads 10% further for four, and tips the two-legged beest. Arena scoring makes C13 a Wednesday must-have.

Q12

Which subdomain and repo name, which host, and should it appear on babissimo.net/projects?

Found: Cloudflare (as Ratchet) can send the headers Q9's option (b) needs; GitHub Pages (as Torus Chess) cannot.

Q13

What will the three pairs use (school laptops, Chromebooks, iPads, their own), in which browser, and is there a printer you can test at 100%?

Found: all timings here are from your Mac. My guess is that a low-end Chromebook runs several times slower, which sets the default population and generations.

Q14

Does a joint switching intersection mid-turn count as a jam, and is there a near-jam margin?

Options: (a) a switch counts as a jam; (b) after the first angle each joint follows the intersection nearest its last position, as a built leg would, and the sim flags where that differs from the max-y/min-x rule; (c) leave the rule as it is. A margin above about 1 mm rejects Jansen (§1). Also: is 180 angles, as in your crossover test, fine enough?

Q4

What tolerance counts as "down" for stride and time down, and can students change it?

Found: stride runs from 50 to 68 across tolerances of 0.5 to 4 (§1), so Tuesday's "beat Jansen by hand" number depends on it.

Q7

Does the four-bar mode offer an offset tracing point whatever the build uses, and is it set as a distance along the coupler plus a sideways offset, or as two triangle sides?

Found: waiting on your build test; the sim can do either.

Q15

What does fitness receive, and what is it called?

Found: the slides use fitness(leg) with leg.jams, leg.stride and leg.step_height, but scoring is per beest. Keep leg and hang the beest measures on it, rename it beest, or pass both? And "flatness" is not yet defined: the foot's height range while down (depends on Q4), or body bob (needs no tolerance)?

Needed by Saturday afternoon

Q9

How do students edit and run code, and what happens on an error or an endless loop?

Options for Stop: (a) the GA runs in a worker that Stop terminates, then restarts in a few seconds, on any host; (b) Pyodide's interrupt, which needs cross-origin isolation headers (Q12); (c) a time check inside the GA loop, which cannot stop a loop inside student code. On an error: show the traceback, then halt or carry on with the last code that ran? One editor for both functions or two? Keep each pair's code in the browser?

Q16

How are leg count and crank phase stored and mutated, and which lengths does the stick cap bind?

Found: the slide's random.gauss(0, 3) suits lengths in mm but not a whole number of pairs or an angle. a and l are frame offsets rather than sticks. What range of pairs (one pair tips over), one phase per throw or even spacing, and is there a minimum hole-to-hole length?

Q8

How many mm per Jansen unit, and are genes held in mm or in Jansen units?

Found: Jansen's longest link is h = 65.7, so the 104 mm cap allows at most 1.58 mm per unit for Jansen's own leg, with no headroom left for evolution at that scale. The slides also call normalisation "scaling", which collides with this scale; rename one?

Q5

Where do the fixed per-ingredient limits for the second normalisation come from?

Options: round numbers you pick from Jansen's values; the best found by a single-ingredient search run beforehand; bounds derived from the stick cap.

Q18

With per-generation normalisation, what should the graph plot?

Found: scaling each generation by its own min and max puts the best near 1 every generation, so the graph shows no progress and an elite's score changes as it is carried over. With the first normalisation, Jansen's three-pair body bob is 0.08, so dividing by it magnifies small differences, and flipped scores go negative for beests worse than Jansen. Plot the raw ingredients alongside?

Q6

Which crossover is the default, and is it a setting students compare?

Options: whole-length swap (the slides' "uniform", 25–35% jammed children), blend (12–13%), swap whole triangles (b, d, e and g, i, h travel together), none.

Q17

For Wednesday's comparison, is "the same time" the same seconds or the same number of fitness evaluations, and do the runs overlay on one graph?

Found: seconds favour fast code and vary by device; evaluations are repeatable. A visible, settable random seed would let a pair rerun a result.
